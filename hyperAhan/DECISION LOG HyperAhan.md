# =========================================================== DECISION ID: D-01

Decision: Every product (or product category) must carry a Sale Mode attribute with exactly three defined values: (1) fully online, (2) semi-custom with a default online base configuration plus a phone path for customization, (3) fully custom/contact-only. Reason: The site's purchasing UI (add-to-cart button, contact/request-quote button, or admin-only/no public listing) must be driven by a single, explicit, per-product classification rather than inferred from other properties, because the three sale behaviors described in the business document are structurally different purchase flows, not variations of the same flow. Business Evidence: Document A, Section 1, explicitly defines three categories of steel products by how they are sold: standard products with direct online price and cart purchase; semi-custom products (I-beam) with a default standard-size online path plus a phone path for custom variations; fully custom products (wall-post, bolts) built from raw materials plus ancillary costs, priced only by phone. Document A's own Section 11 executive summary states explicitly: "Every product must have a 'sale mode' characteristic: fully online / online display with phone completion / fully custom-phone." Alternatives Considered: (a) Deriving purchase-flow behavior implicitly from inventory/price-availability state rather than a dedicated field. Rejected because inventory and sale mode were later confirmed (D-13, D-28) to be fully independent concerns; conflating them would have made the fully-custom category indistinguishable from a merely out-of-stock standard product. Confidence: High - directly stated in the primary business document with no ambiguity. Current Status: Confirmed, locked at Step 1 (Ubiquitous Language) and re-confirmed at Step 5 (D-28).

# =========================================================== DECISION ID: D-09

Decision: Tiered/volume pricing discounts are calculated strictly per-order-line-item (per-item), never as a function of the cart's total across multiple different products; and the applicable tier recalculates live/automatically (unlocked) as the customer changes quantity in the cart. Reason: The business owner's own stated design rule is explicit and leaves no room for cart-level aggregation; per-item calculation prevents customers from combining small quantities of different products to improperly reach a wholesale threshold that was intended per product line. Business Evidence: Document A, Section 7-1: "محاسبه‌ی پله بر اساس هر قلم سفارش به‌طور مستقل انجام می‌شود، نه مجموع کل سبد خرید... اگر مشتری هم‌زمان دو محصول متفاوت بخرد که هرکدام کمتر از حد نصاب عمده باشند، تخفیف عمده به هیچ‌کدام تعلق نمی‌گیرد" and "اگر مشتری مقدار سفارش خود را در سبد خرید افزایش دهد... قیمت واحد باید به‌صورت زنده و خودکار به نرخ جدید (عمده) تغییر کند — قیمت پله قفل‌شدنی نیست." Alternatives Considered: (a) Cart-total-based tiering (sum of all line items determines the discount tier). Explicitly and directly rejected by Document A. Confidence: High - explicit, unambiguous, with a worked example directly in the source text. Current Status: Confirmed and re-confirmed as an independent layer at Step 6 (D-22): tier discount rules are defined completely independently of the pricing Formula and are applied on top of the Formula's final output.

# =========================================================== DECISION ID: D-11

Decision: Every price change creates a new, append-only price history record; prior records are never deleted or edited; the system always uses the latest record for display/calculation; a price record can independently be marked "obsolete" so it is excluded from reports, without being deleted. Reason: The business owner requires a durable, auditable price history while also needing a mechanism to exclude certain historical records from reporting without destroying the audit trail - these are explicitly two separate states (deleted vs. obsolete), not one. Business Evidence: Document A, Section 9, in full: "هر تغییر قیمت به‌عنوان یک رکورد جدید ثبت می‌شود؛ رکوردهای قبلی هرگز حذف یا ویرایش نمی‌شوند. سیستم همیشه از آخرین رکورد برای نمایش و محاسبه استفاده می‌کند، اما مدیران باید بتوانند گزارش تاریخی قیمت هر محصول را مشاهده کنند. اما رکورد های قیمت میتوانند منسوخ شوند تا در صورت گرفتن گزارش در آن گنجانده نشوند." Alternatives Considered: (a) Simple mutable price field with no history. Rejected - explicitly contradicted by "prior records are never deleted or edited." (b) Treating "obsolete" as equivalent to soft-deletion with no distinct meaning. Rejected in analysis because the source text explicitly frames obsolescence as a reporting-exclusion mechanism distinct from deletion; this distinction was flagged as important for modeling and treated as a hard requirement, not merged into a single status field without justification. Confidence: High. Current Status: Confirmed, unchanged. Directly reinforced by D-29 (Step 6): the "no price lock, always live" rule works together with this append-only history model - the live-read price is always whichever record is currently latest and non-obsolete.

# =========================================================== DECISION ID: D-13

Decision: Sale Mode (D-01) and the out-of-stock display setting (D-12) are two fully independent attributes on the Product; they must not be collapsed into a single field, and the final catalog display behavior for any given product is the combined result of both independent fields. Reason: A "contact expert" action button can arise from two entirely different causes - a fully-custom Sale Mode (D-01, category 3) or the independent out-of-stock display setting (D-12, state 3) - and conflating these into one field would make it impossible to distinguish "this product is always contact-only by nature" from "this specific unit run happens to be temporarily out of stock and the admin wants a contact button for it." Business Evidence: Synthesized directly from Document A Section 1 (sale mode categories) and Section 3 (inventory display), which describe two structurally separate business concerns using similar surface-level UI language ("contact/تماس بگیرید"), combined with the business owner's explicit, separate answers to Sale Mode questions (Step 1 analysis) versus Inventory questions (Step 5, D-12) - at no point did the business owner indicate these two concerns should be merged. Alternatives Considered: (a) A single combined "display state" enum covering both sale-mode-driven and inventory-driven contact scenarios in one field. This was the analysis team's own internally raised concern in the initial document review ("این دو بعد به یک اکشن مشابه ختم می‌شوند... همین باعث شده تناقض ایجاد شود") and was explicitly flagged as a question requiring confirmation rather than assumed; the business owner's subsequent independent, separate answers to Sale Mode and Inventory questions (never merging them) served as the de facto confirmation that these remain two fields. Confidence: High - modeling conclusion drawn from consistently separate treatment of the two concerns across the entire conversation, with no business owner statement ever merging them. Current Status: Confirmed, locked at Step 5, and directly incorporated into the Product aggregate design (D-15).

# =========================================================== DECISION ID: D-15

(b) A single generic aggregate with a structured, category-dependent Specification value object. Selected. Confidence: High - directly requested confirmation was obtained from the business owner before this was locked, elevating what began as a medium-confidence inference (initially proposed as "gezine-ye alef" during Step 3 discussion) to a fully confirmed decision. Current Status: Confirmed and locked at Step 3, re-confirmed via D-14 in the same exchange. Unchanged in all subsequent steps.
